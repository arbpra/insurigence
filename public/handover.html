What's been built

In the order you'd move through it.

Quote entry

  • The screen where you record what the carriers sent back — add, edit, delete, reorder, and mark one option as recommended.
  • Every field from your requirement list — carrier, program, policy type, all three date types, premium, taxes, fees, payment plan, limits, deductibles, endorsements, exclusions, and notes. Validated as you type, with money handled in exact cents so nothing drifts by a penny.
  • The total adds itself up, but a total you type in always wins. Carriers sometimes quote a figure that doesn't equal its parts, and theirs is the number that belongs in front of the insured.
  • Carrier quote documents attach to the option. Nothing is publicly reachable — a download link is minted on demand and dies after five minutes.
  • An expired quote is badged as expired, so a stale option can't quietly make it into a proposal.

Plain-language coverage explanations

  • Each coverage explained in plain language, drafted for you and fully editable before it goes anywhere.
  • The AI can never touch a number. Limits and deductibles are yours alone; it writes wording only. Its output has no field for a limit or a premium, so a wrong figure has no route to reach your client — this is built into the structure, not merely asked of the model.
  • Anything drafted for you stays flagged until an agent reviews it, and re-drafting never overwrites wording you've edited yourself.
  • Editing text you'd already approved returns it to pending, so a late change can't slip out unreviewed.

Comparison and recommendation

  • Side-by-side comparison with the differences highlighted, and a filter to show only what actually differs.
  • Coverages match across carriers despite naming differences, and limits are read however they're written — $250,000, 250K, $1M/$2M.
  • A coverage missing from an option reads "Not quoted", which is deliberately distinct from "Not included". The system never guesses which one you meant.
  • "Why we recommend this option" is drafted from the same engine that draws the table, so the wording can never contradict the figures beside it.

The proposal builder

  • All nine proposal sections, reorderable and retitleable, with a live preview beside the editor that autosaves as you work.
  • Required sections can't be removed, so a proposal can never go out with no options in it.
  • Your branding throughout — logo, colours, footer, and contact details.
  • The disclaimer follows a clear order: platform default, your agency's own version, then frozen onto the proposal the moment it's sent — so revising it later never changes what someone has already signed.
  • Anything sent to a client is built from an approved list of fields, so a field added in future stays private until someone deliberately publishes it.

Sending it

  • Send, copy link, resend, and withdraw — withdrawal takes effect immediately.
  • Links are random and unguessable, unrelated to anything else in the system, and expire after 60 days.
  • Resending reuses the same link, so one already sitting in your client's inbox keeps working.
  • The email carries no premiums, carriers, or limits. A forwarded email shouldn't disclose someone's pricing, so everything sensitive stays behind the link.
  • If the email fails, the link is still yours. You're told the link is ready but the message didn't send, rather than losing both.

What your client sees

  • They open their proposal with no account and no password.
  • Built for the phone first — options stack as cards rather than a wide table, because these links are almost always opened from email on a phone.
  • Coverage detail sits inside each option, next to the price it belongs to, rather than in a separate section further down the page.
  • They select an option, confirm, and can change their mind any time before signing. Selecting is explicitly not signing, and the screen says so.
  • Fixed along the way: the client page had been sitting behind the login wall, so every insured opening their link was bounced to a sign-in screen. It had been broken since the feature was first built.

Electronic signature

  • Typed or drawn. The drawing pad takes finger, stylus, or mouse, and scales to the screen so a phone signature isn't exported blurred.
  • Consent is required, and stored word-for-word with each signature. If the wording is revised later, existing signatures still show exactly what was agreed.
  • Full audit record: IP address, device, timestamp, proposal version, and the consent text.
  • The signed document is frozen. A complete snapshot is taken at the moment of signing, so editing the underlying quote afterwards cannot change what your client agreed to.
  • An option must be chosen before signing, so every signature attaches to a specific quote, and a typed signature has to match the legal name entered above it.

This is the signature described in your specification — typed or drawn, with a complete audit record. It is not a certified third-party e-signature service. If DocuSign-grade certification is required, please flag it: that is a different build.

PDF documents

  • The full proposal as a branded PDF, paginated properly with repeating table headers and page numbers.
  • The signed version adds a signature block and a certification page carrying the audit record, with every time stated in UTC.
  • Download from both sides — you from the dashboard, your client from their own link.
  • Which version you get is decided by the proposal's state, never by a setting, so an unsigned copy of a signed document can't be handed out by mistake.
  • Internal notes can never reach a client's PDF. The document is built from the client-facing view only.

Tracking and notifications

  • Twelve kinds of event recorded across the whole lifecycle — created, sent, resent, opened, viewed, option selected, option changed, signed, PDF generated, link withdrawn, new version, and expired.
  • An E-Sign activity log showing that timeline, including the signature audit record.
  • You're emailed on first open, on selection, and on signature. Only the first open — repeat reads aren't news.
  • Lead status keeps itself current and never moves backwards; reopening a proposal after signing cannot undo it.
  • A signature takes a lead to "Ready to Bind" — never to "Bound". Insurigence does not bind coverage; that stays your decision once the carrier confirms.
  • A lapsed proposal stops reading as "Sent", so your list stays honest.

Versioning and security

  • A draft is edited in place. A proposal your client already holds a link to is forked into a new version instead of being overwritten — and the old link keeps serving exactly what they were sent.
  • Signed versions are never editable, and every earlier version stays in the history.
  • Every screen is scoped to its own agency. No agency can reach another's leads, proposals, quotes, or documents — and a request for something outside your agency is answered as though it doesn't exist, rather than confirming it's there.
  • Every client link passes through one security gate, so withdrawal and expiry can't be skipped by any route.
  • The page sent to a client withholds internal notes, scoring, and file locations — enforced by test, not by convention.
